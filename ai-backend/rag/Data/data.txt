Q: What are the common signs of depression?
A: Common signs of depression include persistent sadness, loss of interest in activities, changes in appetite or sleep patterns, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, feelings of worthlessness or guilt, and thoughts of death or suicide. These symptoms must persist for at least two weeks and significantly impact daily functioning.

Q: How can I help someone who is having a panic attack?
A: Stay calm and reassuring, help them focus on slow, deep breathing, remind them that the panic attack will pass, avoid saying "calm down" or minimizing their experience, stay with them until they feel better, and encourage them to seek professional help if attacks are frequent.

Q: What is the difference between sadness and depression?
A: Sadness is a normal emotional response to specific events and typically passes with time. Depression is a persistent mental health condition lasting weeks or months, affecting multiple areas of life including sleep, appetite, energy, and the ability to function normally.

Q: When should someone seek professional mental health help?
A: Seek help when mental health symptoms persist for more than two weeks, interfere with daily activities, relationships, or work, include thoughts of self-harm, involve substance abuse as coping, or when family and friends express concern about changes in behavior.

Q: What are healthy coping strategies for stress?
A: Healthy coping strategies include regular exercise, maintaining a consistent sleep schedule, practicing mindfulness or meditation, talking to trusted friends or family, engaging in hobbies, limiting caffeine and alcohol, setting realistic goals, and seeking professional support when needed.

Q: How can I support a friend with anxiety?
A: Listen without judgment, validate their feelings, learn about anxiety to better understand their experience, encourage professional help if needed, be patient with their limitations, avoid giving unsolicited advice, and maintain your own boundaries and self-care.
